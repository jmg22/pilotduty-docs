PilotDuty records your flights without you touching the app. Once your aircraft are set up and tracking is on, it monitors your activity: it recognizes taxi, takeoff, landing and parking, then places the flight in your approval queue.

## Events and flights

The app automatically creates **events** when you enter or leave a tracked zone. There are three types of events:

- **Flight event.** Created when the app detects that you entered an aircraft's zone. A flight event contains one or more **flights**, with all their details: start of taxi (off-block), takeoff, landing, arrival at parking (on-block), aircraft information, and so on.
- **Work event.** With PilotDuty Pro. Created when you enter a work area (iBeacon or GPS zone) and you are not flying.
- **Rest event.** With PilotDuty Pro. Created when you enter a rest area (iBeacon or GPS zone), usually during your off-duty time.

> Aircraft zones always come first. With duty time turned on, if a flight event starts because you entered an aircraft's zone but you don't fly, the app converts it into a work event.

Every event then waits for your approval on the Dashboard. See [Review and approval](/en/docs/app/review-and-approval).

## Tracked zones

The app starts following your activity when your iPhone enters a tracked zone, and stops when it leaves. Aircraft zones come in two kinds:

- **An aerodrome.** A zone around the aerodrome, located by GPS.
- **An aircraft fitted with an iBeacon.** The app knows you are on board when it picks up the beacon. Beacon-triggered recording requires a PilotDuty+ or PilotDuty Pro subscription. See [iBeacon](/en/docs/app/ibeacon).

An aircraft without a beacon can also be set up for manual start. See below.

With PilotDuty Pro and duty time turned on, you can also define a work area and a rest area. See [Duty time](/en/docs/app/duty-time).

Your aircraft and aircraft zones are in the Aircraft tab (airplane icon). To add one, see [Adding an aircraft](/en/docs/app/ibeacon).

Only one zone is active at a time. When several apply, an iBeacon comes before GPS, and an aircraft zone comes before a work area, which comes before a rest area. These zones require location set to “Always”.

## The steps recorded

In an aircraft's zone, the app follows your GPS speed and notes four times:

1. start of taxi (off-block);
2. takeoff;
3. landing;
4. arrival at parking (on-block).

The flight's durations are calculated from these times. The app also picks the nearest aerodrome and works out whether the flight took place by day or by night. It notes the GPS positions of the takeoff and landing when they are accurate enough. They are used to draw the flight on the logbook map.

While recording, the dashboard shows a progress bar and the zone's name. The flight in progress appears with an animated indicator and running timers.

Once the aircraft is parked, the app waits for the parking time limit before closing the flight. The flight then joins the approval queue, with its confidence score. See [Review and approval](/en/docs/app/review-and-approval).

## Setting the parking time limit

The parking time limit (“Parking time limit”) keeps a flight from being split into several entries during a short stop or a touch-and-go. With it, PilotDuty keeps logging a single flight when the aircraft stops briefly and is quickly airborne again.

### What the limit does

It is a threshold, in seconds, that you choose. It sets how long the aircraft can stay stopped before PilotDuty ends the current flight and starts a new one.

- If the aircraft is stopped for **less** than the limit, the current flight continues.
- If the aircraft is stopped for **longer** than the limit, a new flight is created.

> Example: with a limit of 30 seconds, any stop shorter than 30 seconds does **not** create a new flight.

![Touch-and-go diagram: the aircraft lands, stops briefly on the runway during the parking time limit, then takes off again](/docs-media/app/automatic-recording-parking-time-limit-touch-and-go.png)

In this example, the aircraft lands, stops briefly and takes off again. If the stop is shorter than the parking time limit, the app logs a single flight.

### Setting the limit

1. Tap the Settings tab (gear icon).
2. Choose General.
3. Scroll down to “Flight overlap threshold (in seconds)”.
4. Enter the value you want in “Parking time limit”.

The welcome flow sets this limit to 30 seconds.

![General settings: an arrow points down to the “Flight overlap threshold” section and the “Parking time limit” field set to 30](/docs-media/app/automatic-recording-parking-time-limit-setting.png)

### Best practices

- For **training circuits** or **touch-and-go exercises**, increase the limit, for example to 60 seconds.
- For **commercial operations**, keep the default, for example 30 seconds, to separate flights clearly.

> With PilotDuty Pro: this setting only applies to flights detected in aircraft zones. It does not affect work or rest area events.

The parking time limit gives you control over how flights are logged during short stops. Adjust it to your flying to reduce manual corrections in your logbook.

## Without a network

Detection works without a network: the flight is saved on your iPhone, then sent to the server when you approve it and a connection is available. Flight following and text messages to your follower do need a network.

## Aircraft without a beacon: starting and stopping a session by hand

If you don't use a beacon, set up the aircraft with the “User initiated” initiator in the Aircraft tab. See [Adding an aircraft](/en/docs/app/ibeacon). You then start and stop your flights yourself.

For an aircraft set up for manual start, the dashboard shows a start button:

1. Tap the start button.
2. Choose the aircraft from the list.
3. At the end of the flight, tap the stop button.

This button only appears if at least one of your aircraft is set up for manual start.

## Tips

- **Keep location on “Always”** and leave “Precise Location” on in iOS Settings. See [Getting started](/en/docs/app/getting-started).
- **Allow Motion access.** It helps the app tell taxiing from stopping. Without it, the app relies on GPS alone.
- **Don't force-quit the app**, meaning don't swipe it away in the app switcher, especially before or during a flight. The app has to stay active in the background to follow the flight.
- **Check that tracking is on** (“GPS Tracking”) in the app's general settings.
- **Read the dashboard banner.** It flags anything that can get in the way of recording: GPS, network, permissions, Low Power Mode, an available update. Tap it to see what to do.

## If a flight is missing or looks wrong

- You can add a missed flight by hand, or read it from a cockpit screen with the camera. See [Manual entry and ACARS scan](/en/docs/app/manual-entry-and-acars-scan).
- With PilotDuty Pro, the app can recover a flight from ADS-B data. See [ADS-B recovery](/en/docs/app/ads-b-recovery).
- If GPS missed the moment of a step, the app may suggest an estimated time during review.
- A flight detected by mistake can be deleted with a swipe to the left in the approval queue.
