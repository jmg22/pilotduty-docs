PilotDuty keeps your logbook for you. The app recognizes your taxi, takeoffs and landings, then gives you each flight to review before it goes into your logbook.

## What the app does

- It records your flights on its own, using your iPhone's GPS or an iBeacon placed in the aircraft.
- It lets you review, correct and approve each flight.
- It keeps your logbook: list, filters, map, currency and totals.
- With a subscription, it exports your logbook, imports your old logbooks, keeps someone informed during your flights, and tracks your duty time and your documents (medical, licenses, competencies).
- If you belong to an organization that uses PilotDuty Manager, your approved flights show up there and you receive its memos.

You can use the app on your own, without an organization.

## Install the app

Download PilotDuty to your iPhone from the App Store. The app is available in English and French.

## Create your account

The first time you open the app, a short welcome flow guides you. You create your account in one of three ways:

- with an email address and a password (you must accept the terms of use);
- with your Apple ID;
- with your Google account.

If your email address already has an account, the app switches to sign-in on its own.

If you belong to an organization, use the same account as in PilotDuty Manager. The app and Manager share the same database.

## Choose your setup

The app then asks how you plan to record your flights:

- **With an aircraft.** You say where the aircraft comes from: your organization's aircraft, an aircraft with a beacon, or an aircraft without one. The app takes you to the right step: picking an organization aircraft, adopting the beacon, or setting up the aircraft.
- **With your airline roster.** You connect the iCal link your employer provides. This choice requires PilotDuty Pro. See [Airline roster](/en/docs/app/airline-roster).
- **Later.** You skip this step and set up the app whenever you like.

## Grant permissions

To record your flights, the app asks for three permissions:

- **Location.** Choose “Always”: the app must follow your position in the background, even with the screen locked. Also leave “Precise Location” on. With an approximate location, the app cannot detect your flights.
- **Motion.** The app uses the iPhone's motion sensors to recognize taxiing more reliably. Without this permission, it relies on GPS alone.
- **Notifications.** The app lets you know, for example, when a memo arrives or when it has rebuilt a flight.

If you chose the airline roster, the app only asks for notifications. A screen is skipped if you have already answered that question.

You can change these permissions later in iOS Settings. The app shows their status in its general settings. See [Settings and feedback](/en/docs/app/settings-and-feedback).

## Your profile

The welcome flow also presents the plans. The last step asks for your name, your ratings (CPL, IFR, multi-engine, seaplane, instructor) and your total hours before PilotDuty. That total is the starting point of your career total. You can break it down later; see [Logbook](/en/docs/app/logbook). You then receive a welcome email.

## The free version limit

The free version counts up to 3 flights per month. Beyond that, the dashboard shows the subscription screen instead of your flights to approve. The limit does not apply if you belong to an organization that subscribes to PilotDuty Manager. See [Subscriptions](/en/docs/app/subscriptions).

## Getting help

Help bubbles appear as you use the app, for example to show the swipe gestures. In the Settings tab, the “i” button opens a help video.

If the app shows a required-update screen, install the latest version from the App Store.
