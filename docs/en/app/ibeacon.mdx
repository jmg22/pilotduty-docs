An iBeacon placed in the aircraft lets the app know you are on board. Flight recording starts when your iPhone picks up the beacon.

Beacon-triggered recording requires PilotDuty+ or PilotDuty Pro.

## Adding an aircraft

Follow these steps to add an aircraft to your PilotDuty logbook and set it up for automatic flight detection with an iBeacon. Without a beacon, you can also set the aircraft up for manual start. Have the aircraft's information ready so you can add it without delay.

### Step 1 – Open the Aircraft tab

In the bottom bar, tap the Aircraft tab (airplane icon). It shows the list of your tracked aircraft (“Tracked Aircraft”).

### Step 2 – Tap “+”

At the top right of the “Tracked Aircraft” screen, tap “+” to create an aircraft.

As long as no aircraft is tracked, the screen reminds you that you need a compatible beacon to get the most out of automatic recording, with a “Buy an iBeacon” button.

![Empty “Tracked Aircraft” screen: an arrow points to the “+” button at the top right, above the “Improve your flight tracking experience” message and the “Buy an iBeacon” button](/docs-media/app/ibeacon-tracked-aircraft-add.png)

### Step 3 – Enter the aircraft's information

Fill in the following fields:

- **“Initiator”.** Choose “User initiated” if you don't use a beacon, or “iBeacon” if you do. You will assign it in the next step.
- **“CallSign”.** The registration, for example C-FFFF. Required.
- **“Aircraft Type”.** The model, for example C172. Required.
- **“Takeoff speed (Knots)”.** The aircraft's average rotation speed, in knots. The app uses it to recognize the takeoff.

Then define the aircraft's configuration:

- **“Aircraft Type Category”.** The landing gear category, for example “Tricycle Train”.
- **“Engine Type”.** The engine, for example “Mono Piston”.
- **“Propeller Type”.** The propeller, for example “Fixed pitch”.
- **IFR and “Single Pilot”.** Turn these switches on or off according to the aircraft's capabilities: instrument flight, single-pilot operation.

The app also asks for your status on board. An aircraft picture adapts to the category and number of engines. This information is used to fill in the flights recorded with this aircraft.

![“Edit Area” screen: “Initiator” set to “User initiated”, “CallSign” and “Aircraft Type” fields, “Takeoff speed (Knots)” at 30, category, engine, propeller, IFR and “Single Pilot”](/docs-media/app/ibeacon-edit-aircraft.png)

### Step 4 – Assign a beacon

If you chose “iBeacon” as the initiator:

1. Tap “Adopt an iBeacon”.
2. The app starts scanning for nearby iBeacons.
3. When a beacon is detected close by (“Near”), tap “Adopt Nearest iBeacon”.

If the screen asks for the beacon's identifiers instead, enter its UUID, major and minor.

If no beacon is found, make sure that:

- the batteries are properly installed;
- the protective film is removed;
- you are close to the beacon.

![“iBeacon Detection” screen: beacon detected “Near”, help message, orange “Adopt Nearest iBeacon” button and red “Close” button](/docs-media/app/ibeacon-detection.png)

### Step 5 – Finish

Once every field is filled in:

- the aircraft appears in the “Tracked Aircraft” list;
- you can always come back to change its setup, or assign a beacon later.

![“Tracked Aircraft” list with the aircraft C-FFFF, type C172, and its beacon icon](/docs-media/app/ibeacon-tracked-aircraft-list.png)

You're ready. PilotDuty now uses this aircraft profile to log your flight time: automatically with a beacon, or by hand if you chose “User initiated”. See [Automatic recording](/en/docs/app/automatic-recording).

Don't forget to set your location and notification permissions in the Settings tab, under General, so the app works properly in the background. See [Getting started](/en/docs/app/getting-started).

Adding your aircraft lets you keep a complete record of your flight history.

### Your organization's aircraft

If the aircraft belongs to your organization and a beacon is paired with it in PilotDuty Manager, pick it from your organization's aircraft list instead. The app takes over its setup and turns on beacon detection automatically. See [With your organization](/en/docs/app/with-your-organization).

## Adopting a beacon during the welcome flow

You can also adopt your beacon during the welcome flow:

1. Say that your aircraft has a beacon.
2. Enter the aircraft's registration and type, then adopt the beacon.
3. Set up the aircraft.

These screens are described in detail in [Getting started](/en/docs/app/getting-started).

## The false-flight guard

A beacon sometimes stays in the aircraft on the ground, and your iPhone may pass close by, in a car for example. So that a car ride doesn't turn into a flight, the app requires the beacon's presence to be confirmed before it accepts a takeoff.

- The app checks that the beacon is actually in range before it starts tracking.
- Once airborne, that confirmation holds until you park. A touch-and-go, or losing the beacon signal in flight, does not cut the flight.
- The app learns each beacon's usual signal. That data stays on your iPhone and is never sent anywhere.
- The check happens on the iPhone, without a network.
- If the app doesn't have enough data to decide, it lets the flight through rather than risk missing a real one. You can delete a false flight with a swipe to the left in the approval queue.

## Subscription required

Beacon-triggered recording requires a PilotDuty+ or PilotDuty Pro subscription. Without a subscription, the beacon does not start recording. See [Subscriptions](/en/docs/app/subscriptions).

## Free beacon with an annual subscription

An annual PilotDuty+ or PilotDuty Pro subscription includes a free iBeacon in the first year.

1. After your purchase, the app asks for your shipping address.
2. If you skip that step, a dashboard banner reminds you that the address is missing.
3. Tap the banner to open the form and enter your address.

![“Shipping Address” screen: address, city, postal code and country fields, and the “Next” button](/docs-media/app/getting-started-shipping-address.png)

## Good to know

Takeoff and landing are always detected by the iPhone's GPS. Keep location on “Always” and leave “Precise Location” on. See [Automatic recording](/en/docs/app/automatic-recording).
