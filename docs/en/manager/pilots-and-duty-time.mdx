The Pilots module holds each member's file: medical, competencies, logbook, statistics and duty time benchmarks.

## Who can do it

- Every member sees their own file. Only the pilot edits their logbook, medical and initial times.
- Instructor and above: the full member list and other members' complete files (medical, competencies, duty, logbook).
- Chief Instructor and above: add a competency issued by the organization, pair trainers and students.

## The pilot profile

Open the Pilots menu. The list offers four views (everyone, my profile, my students and trainers) and a search by name, email or phone. The shield in the status column sums up medical and competency validity: green, yellow, red, or gray when there is no information.

A profile has four tabs: overview, duty, flights and bookings.

## Medical and competencies

- Medical: the pilot adds their own certificate (TC, FAA, EASA or other). The expiry date is calculated automatically for TC, FAA and EASA. The certificate photo must be an image.
- Licences and competencies: licences, ratings, checks and courses, with where each one comes from. A Chief Instructor and above can add a competency issued by the organization. The pilot declares their other licences in the iPhone app (PilotDuty Pro personal subscription).

Every certificate and competency shows a countdown: expires in N days, expires today, expired N days ago, or no expiry. The badge turns orange 30 days before expiry (90 days for a licence or a student permit), then red.

## Logbook

On the duty tab, the event list shows work, flight and rest days with their flights. It is the same logbook as in the iPhone app: a flight approved in the app shows up here.

- The flights tab lists flights and lets you add one.
- Disapproving an event moves it to the archives, where you can restore it.
- The history shows who changed what, and why: every change creates a version. The integrity check verifies the chain and reports it as intact, broken at a given version, or incomplete.

## Create events and flights

You can manually create work, flight and rest events, then add your flight details to them. You can only enter events in your own logbook.

> The screenshots in this section date from 2025, when the logbook had its own “Events” menu entry. It is now in your pilot file. The “Delete Event” button in the screenshots has become disapprove event: the event moves to the archives instead of being deleted.

### Step 1: open the event creation window

1. Open the Pilots menu, then your own profile. On the duty tab, open the event list.
2. Click “New Event” at the top of the list.

![Former “Events” page from 2025, with the “New Event” button highlighted](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-new-event-2025.png)

### Step 2: choose the event type

In the “Create Event” window, open the “Event Type” list and choose:

- “Work/Flight Event”, the default;
- “Quick Flight Duty Event”;
- “Rest Event”.

![“Create Event” window: the “Event Type” list open, with Work/Flight Event, Quick Flight Duty Event and Rest Event](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-event-type.png)

### Step 3: set the event time

1. Select the “Event Start Date” and the “Event Stop Date”.
2. Depending on the type, also enter the fleet aircraft, operating rules, flight rules, number of flights, total flight time, crew and your pilot status. The flights created are spread evenly over the period, with “Unknown” airports to complete.
3. Click “Create Event” to proceed.

![“Create Event” window: the Work/Flight Event type, the start and stop dates and the “Create Event” button](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-create-event.png)

Once created, the event appears in the list. If the flight time is longer than the event, the event is created without flights.

### Step 4: edit the event (optional)

1. Click the event in the list.
2. In the “Edit Event” column, you can change:
   - the event type, for example switch between work, flight and rest;
   - the start and stop time;
   - the remark.
3. Don't forget to click “Save” after making changes.

![“Edit Event” column: the event type list open, with Work Event, Flight Event and Rest Event](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-edit-event.jpg)

### Step 5: add a flight to a flight event

A flight event can hold one or more flights.

1. Click “New Flight” and select the aircraft.

![Flight event: the “New Flight” button and the C-FFFF aircraft choice highlighted](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-new-flight.jpg)

2. The “Edit Flight” column opens on the right.
3. Fill in the flight details:
   - “Call Sign” and “Aircraft Type”;
   - the departure and arrival airports;
   - the “OffBlock”, “TakeOff”, “Landing” and “OnBlock” times;
   - “T/L Cycles”;
   - the flight rules, night flight, operating rules, flight type and “Single Pilot?”;
   - your pilot status.
4. Attach photos, PDFs or other files if needed.
5. Click “Save Flight” once done.

![Filled-in “Edit Flight” column: C-FFFF, C-172, from CYBG to CYQB, the four times, one cycle, VFR and the “Save Flight” button](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-edit-flight.png)

If no flight information is entered, the flight shows as a blank placeholder, with “Unknown” airports:

![Flight event with a blank flight: “From: Unknown” and “To: Unknown”](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-empty-flight.jpg)

### Step 6: sort and filter events

You can sort and filter the event list:

- **Sort order**: by date, ascending or descending.

![Sort list open: Ascending Date and Descending Date](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-sort-order.jpg)

- **Date range**: the current month by default, or this month, last month, the quarter, the year or all. The event type, aircraft and flight rules filters narrow the list.

![Former duration filter from 2025: all events, or the last 7, 28 or 90 days](/docs-media/manager/pilots-and-duty-time-duration-filter-2025.jpg)

In 2025, a duration filter offered the last 7, 28 or 90 days. The date range has replaced it.

You have now created an event and added its flight data in PilotDuty Manager.

### Add a single flight

For a single flight, adding a flight from the flights tab asks for a fleet aircraft or a manual entry, the airports, the four times (off-block, takeoff, landing, on-block), cycles, flight rules, night flight, single pilot and your status. The four times are required. An event is created around the flight, with a 15-minute margin on each side.

## Flight statistics

On the flights tab, flight statistics show total flights, flight, night and duty hours, cycles, and first and last flight, broken down by aircraft type, flight rules and more. Totals update on their own after every flight, edit or import. If something looks off, rebuild the statistics from the logbook settings.

## Duty time: benchmarks only

On the duty tab, duty statistics show duty time, flight time and rest days over 24 hours, 7, 21, 28, 90 and 365 days, with a 365-day timeline. The “Duty Tracking” table is in English. Each total sits next to a fixed Canadian limit, taken from the 702 and 703 rules, with a color code.

- Private flights are excluded from these limits.
- A rest event logged in the logbook counts toward rest and can extend that day's duty limit.

## Initial times and import

- In the logbook settings, enter your hours from before PilotDuty as totals, under initial flight times.
- With the logbook import, bring in your history from LogTen Pro, ForeFlight or MyFlightbook (CSV file up to 10 MB), or photograph a page of your paper logbook (in beta, one page per import).

The import runs on your own profile. It requires your PilotDuty+ or Pro personal subscription, purchased in the iPhone app: the organization's subscription is not enough.

## Trainers and students

An Instructor is a trainer by default. Another member, Pilot and above, becomes one through a trainer authorization: see [Training](/en/docs/manager/training).

A Chief Instructor and above records who trains whom by pairing a trainer and a student; a trainer can also pair a student with themselves. Ending a pairing closes it, and the history is kept. Trainers find their students, with contact details and training records, in the my students view. Students see who is training them under my trainers.

## Good to know

- No medical or competency expiry alert is sent: the countdown is visual only. The shield does not block bookings or assignments.
- Duty statistics are benchmarks: no blocking, no warnings, and the limits cannot be changed. Regulatory duty calculations are done in the iPhone app, with the PilotDuty Pro personal subscription.
- Staff can view a pilot's logbook but cannot edit it.
- A flight added without a fleet aircraft is classified as private, so it is excluded from duty limits.
