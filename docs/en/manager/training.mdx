The Training module tracks each student's training in a pilot training record (PTR): flights, ground school, exercises, exams and progress milestones.

## Who can do it

- Read a record: Pilot and above, and the student for their own record.
- Write in a record and sign a milestone: Instructor, Chief Instructor and above, as well as Pilot and Dispatch members with an active trainer authorization. Never in your own record.
- Co-sign, manage templates and trainer authorizations, transfer a record: Chief Instructor and above.

## Templates

Under Settings › Organization, create PTR templates to describe a program: exercises, milestones, required hours and signature policy. The template editor is in English. A record is always created from one of the organization's templates.

## Open a record

1. On the pilot's profile, in the training records section, create a new PTR.
2. Pick the template. If needed, indicate that the student is continuing from a paper PTR, or pair the student with yourself as trainer.
3. The template's content is frozen into the record.

For a student who arrives with a paper record, a Chief Instructor and above enters their prior hours, ground school and exercises as starting data, so they count toward progress.

## Keep the record

- A flight entry links a logbook flight to the record, with the exercises covered, the student's remarks and the instructor's comments. From the [flight sheet](/en/docs/manager/flight-sheet), you can log the PTR entry, prefilled.
- Add ground school sessions, comments, and the required exams and documents.
- The exercise grid shows progress codes. The audit compares training against the template's requirements, shows a percentage and, when applicable, a ready-for-flight-test badge.
- Add milestones: pre-solo check, first solo authorization, solo cross-country, written exam, pre-flight-test check, course completion, custom milestone, and more.

## Sign a milestone

1. On the milestone, the instructor clicks “E-Sign authorize”.
2. They read the statement, check the exercises, enter their licence and rating, then sign in again (password, Google or Apple).
3. The server records the signer's identity, the exact statement, what was signed and the time, then seals it all with a SHA-256 fingerprint.

A milestone can require a co-signature (“Co-sign”) from a Chief Instructor and above. Before a course completion is signed, the server checks signed milestones, co-signatures, flight and ground hours, and an unexpired medical certificate.

## PDF you can verify by QR code

You can download the PDF of a signed milestone: organization, signer, statement, student, exercises and a QR code. Anyone can scan the code, without an account, to open the verification page. “Signature is authentic” means the record exists and its fingerprint matches.

## Trainer authorizations

Every Instructor is a trainer. For another member, Pilot and above, to train, a Chief Instructor and above opens their profile and grants a trainer authorization: instructor rating, aircraft type, or endorsement (tailwheel, skis, floats, multi-engine, night, IFR, aerobatics), with an expiry date and optional proof. Withdrawing it ends the authorization. The trainers view of the pilot list shows who can train, and until when.

An email goes out 30 days before an authorization ends, to its holder and to each Chief Instructor and above, in each person's language.

## Close or transfer a record

A record can be closed as completed, with the option to add a competency to the pilot's file. It can also be marked failed or withdrawn. For a student moving to another organization, a Chief Instructor and above creates a transfer link valid for 30 days and sends it themselves.

## Good to know

- Not everything is signed. Flight entries, ground school, comments, progress codes, exams, closing a record and the competency added at closing are recorded without a signature.
- The electronic signature is a sealed server-side record, verifiable by QR code. It is not a digital certificate, and the signer's licence is self-declared, not checked.
- Statements and signed PDFs are in English.
- The ready-for-flight-test badge is an indicator: it neither authorizes nor blocks anything.
- A trainer authorization is a designation by the organization, not a regulatory check.
- A transfer creates a new record at the receiving organization, without the entries, milestones, signatures or documents.
