The Fleet module brings together your aircraft, their hours and cycles, their maintenance program, their inspections and their snags. It keeps you informed: it never blocks a booking or a flight.

## Who can do it

- View the fleet and snags: all members.
- Create, duplicate or delete an aircraft, manage inspections and workshops: Operations Manager and Maintenance Manager.
- Edit an aircraft's record (inspection schedules, items): Chief Instructor and above.
- Report a snag, comment on it, add photos: Pilot and above.
- Rectify a snag and sign the return to service: Maintenance Manager, Co-owner and Operations Manager.

## Add an aircraft

1. Open the aircraft list and start a new aircraft.
2. Go through the wizard: identity, aircraft type, operations, counters, maintenance, booking and summary. At the counters step, enter the current airframe time and cycles.
3. Create the aircraft.

The booking step (seats, fuel, hourly rate, empty weight and arm) only appears for the Operations Manager. An aircraft created without it can be made bookable later: see [Bookings](/en/docs/manager/bookings).

The final screen lists what is left to do, including having pilots import the aircraft into the iPhone app. Write it down: the screen disappears when you leave the page. Duplicating an aircraft copies its configuration and maintenance program, but the copy is not bookable.

## The generic maintenance program

At the maintenance step, PilotDuty suggests a generic program that fits the aircraft's profile: inspections (100-hour/annual, 50-hour, or annual for a glider) and common items. You can also start from an empty list.

Each interval must be confirmed by the person your authority designates: your AME, an A&P or IA, or your CAMO/CAO. For a helicopter, the suggested program is minimal.

## Edit an aircraft

In the aircraft list, open the row's action menu and choose to edit the aircraft. The page has five tabs: info, inspection schedules, items by hours, items by date and items by cycles. Each field is saved on its own when you leave it.

The info tab holds the aircraft's identity and configuration: type, callsign, initial airframe time and cycles, make, model, serial numbers, category, engine, propeller, flight type, IFR, single pilot and operating rules. It also holds:

- **Reference links**: your links to airworthiness directives, service bulletins, service difficulty reports, the annual airworthiness information report, and the airframe, engine and propeller publications. The directive and service difficulty links open from an inspection's pre-check.
- **The iBeacon**: its serial number, entered by hand or read from its QR code, and the takeoff speed, in knots.

## Hours, cycles and due dates

Total airframe time and total airframe cycles update on their own from flights flown by members of the organization.

- Inspection schedules: a recurring inspection by date, by hours, by cycles or a combination, with an interval, a window and a tolerance. On the inspection schedules tab, you can upload the inspection's task file: the aircraft page then offers it for download.
- Items by hours, by date and by cycles: each component gets its own interval (engine overhaul, propeller, ELT, calibrations, life-limited parts).

On each items tab, add a line for each new item: item, task, last completion (airframe hours, date or airframe cycles at that time), interval and tolerance. Check the on-condition box for an item maintained according to its condition rather than at a fixed interval. The row's action menu deletes the item.

Items are color-coded: in service, due for inspection, on condition, overdue, or to complete. An item whose last completion or interval is unknown stays “to complete”: no due date is ever made up. The fleet list shows what remains before each aircraft's most restrictive inspection.

## Inspections

1. For each inspection type, start by creating an initial inspection: the last inspection performed and the next target. Enter the date, then, depending on the inspection, the target date, hours or cycles. Repeat for each inspection, longest interval first: the button to start an inspection only appears afterward. Do not leave a target empty: it would count as 0 and the inspection would be overdue at once.
2. Starting an inspection opens four steps: pre-check, workshop, items and finalize. When you submit, the work order and the status report are generated as PDFs, and the next due date moves forward.
   - Pre-check: the inspection suggested from the due dates, or a custom task for a work order outside an inspection, such as the periodic check of an altimeter, a transponder or an ELT. Enter the date, hours and cycles, then check the four reviews: airworthiness directives, service difficulty reports, technical publications and recurring defects.
   - Workshop: a workshop from your list, or the owner instead. The details can be changed for this inspection only.
   - Items: check the items done during the inspection. Overdue, due and on-condition items are offered; you can show all the others.
   - Finalize: review the summary, then submit.
3. Once the work is done, finalize the pending inspection. Attach, if needed, the work report, the inspection task and the quality control report from the workshop. Check the items completed and confirm the technical log was reviewed, then finalize. The alert card is generated as a PDF. Only the checked items move forward.

An inspection also resets the inspections with a shorter interval. A custom task changes none of them.

Only one inspection can be pending at a time. Any member can download the latest alert card.

The three PDFs:

- **Work order**: the organization, the aircraft, the targets, the workshop, the inspection requested and the items to do, with a signature line for the person responsible for maintenance (PRM). While the inspection is pending, you can download it from the inspection tile.
- **Status report**: the status of the aircraft's inspection schedules and items. You can also download it while the inspection is pending.
- **Alert card**: total airframe time, cycles, last inspection, workshop, next inspections and the items that are overdue or due for inspection.

The inspection history lists past inspections. Each inspection's details give access to its files.

## Snags

1. On the aircraft page, open the snags tab and report a snag: description, severity (No-Go, Go-If or Info), optional ATA chapter and location. Add photos (up to 10) and comments.
2. The maintenance lead marks the snag in progress, then enters the rectification: corrective action, labor, parts and mechanic.
3. They then sign the return to service and sign in again. Their electronic signature is recorded and sealed with a fingerprint shown on the snag. The snag moves to closed.

Snags only move forward: open, in progress, rectified, closed. A snag cannot be deleted. An email goes out when a snag is reported (to maintenance leads), when it is rectified (to the reporter) and when it is signed off.

## Workshops

Under Settings › Organization, keep your list of workshops (AMO or AME). It is offered at each inspection. Workshops have no access to PilotDuty.

1. Add a workshop.
2. For an approved maintenance organization, check the AMO box, then enter the AMO name, the PRM name and the AMO number. Otherwise, enter the AME name and number.
3. Enter the address, phone number and email, which are all required, then save.

A workshop's action menu lets you edit or delete it. The first workshop in the list is preselected at an inspection's workshop step, and each inspection keeps its own copy of the workshop's details.

## Good to know

- The module blocks nothing. A No-Go snag or an overdue item does not take the aircraft off the booking calendar. To block it, add an unavailability to the calendar: see [Bookings](/en/docs/manager/bookings).
- No due-date alert is sent, by email or text message. Due dates are shown on screen.
- Everything is counted in airframe hours and cycles. There is no separate engine or propeller counter.
- Inspection PDFs are in English. Inspections are not signed electronically.
- MEL item deferral is still in development.
- An inspection schedule cannot be renamed: deleting and recreating it erases its due dates.
