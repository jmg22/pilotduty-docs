The Fleet module brings together your aircraft, their hours and cycles, their maintenance program, their inspections and their snags. It keeps you informed: it never blocks a booking or a flight.

## Who can do it

- View the fleet and snags: all members.
- Create, duplicate or delete an aircraft, manage inspections and workshops: Operations Manager and Maintenance Manager.
- Edit an aircraft's record (inspection schedules, items): Chief Instructor and above.
- Report a snag, comment on it, add photos: Pilot and above.
- Rectify a snag and sign the return to service: Maintenance Manager, Co-owner and Operations Manager.

## Add an aircraft

1. Open the aircraft list and start a new aircraft.
2. Go through the wizard: identity, aircraft type, operations, counters, maintenance, booking and summary. At the counters step, enter the current airframe time and cycles.
3. Create the aircraft.

The booking step (seats, fuel, hourly rate, empty weight and arm) only appears for the Operations Manager. An aircraft created without it can be made bookable later: see [Bookings](/en/docs/manager/bookings).

The final screen lists what is left to do, including having pilots import the aircraft into the iPhone app. Write it down: the screen disappears when you leave the page. Duplicating an aircraft copies its configuration and maintenance program, but the copy is not bookable.

## The generic maintenance program

At the maintenance step, PilotDuty suggests a generic program that fits the aircraft's profile: inspections (100-hour/annual, 50-hour, or annual for a glider) and common items. You can also start from an empty list.

Each interval must be confirmed by the person your authority designates: your AME, an A&P or IA, or your CAMO/CAO. For a helicopter, the suggested program is minimal.

## Hours, cycles and due dates

Total airframe time and total airframe cycles update on their own from flights flown by members of the organization.

- Inspection schedules: a recurring inspection by date, by hours, by cycles or a combination, with an interval, a window and a tolerance.
- Items by hours, by date and by cycles: each component gets its own interval (engine overhaul, propeller, ELT, calibrations, life-limited parts).

Items are color-coded: in service, due for inspection, on condition, overdue, or to complete. An item whose last completion or interval is unknown stays “to complete”: no due date is ever made up. The fleet list shows what remains before each aircraft's most restrictive inspection.

## Inspections

1. For each inspection type, start by creating an initial inspection: the last inspection performed and the next target.
2. Starting an inspection opens four steps: pre-check, workshop, items and finalize. When you submit, the work order and the status report are generated as PDFs, and the next due date moves forward.
3. Once the work is done, finalize the pending inspection. Check the items completed and confirm the technical log was reviewed. The alert card is generated as a PDF.

Only one inspection can be pending at a time. Any member can download the latest alert card.

## Snags

1. On the aircraft page, open the snags tab and report a snag: description, severity (No-Go, Go-If or Info), optional ATA chapter and location. Add photos (up to 10) and comments.
2. The maintenance lead marks the snag in progress, then enters the rectification: corrective action, labor, parts and mechanic.
3. They then sign the return to service and sign in again. Their electronic signature is recorded and sealed with a fingerprint shown on the snag. The snag moves to closed.

Snags only move forward: open, in progress, rectified, closed. A snag cannot be deleted. An email goes out when a snag is reported (to maintenance leads), when it is rectified (to the reporter) and when it is signed off.

## Workshops

Under Settings › Organization, keep your list of workshops (AMO or AME). It is offered at each inspection. Workshops have no access to PilotDuty.

## Good to know

- The module blocks nothing. A No-Go snag or an overdue item does not take the aircraft off the booking calendar. To block it, add an unavailability to the calendar: see [Bookings](/en/docs/manager/bookings).
- No due-date alert is sent, by email or text message. Due dates are shown on screen.
- Everything is counted in airframe hours and cycles. There is no separate engine or propeller counter.
- Inspection PDFs are in English. Inspections are not signed electronically.
- MEL item deferral is still in development.
- An inspection schedule cannot be renamed: deleting and recreating it erases its due dates.
